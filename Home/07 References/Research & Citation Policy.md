# Purpose

This document defines the research methodology, source hierarchy, citation standards, translation principles, commentary policy, and editorial research practices followed throughout the **Sanātana Dharma Dharohar** repository.

Its purpose is to ensure that every permanent note is based on authentic, verifiable, and transparent sources rather than opinion, popularity, or unsupported claims.

This policy applies to all present and future contributors.

---

# Guiding Philosophy

Sanātana Dharma is a living spiritual tradition preserved through scripture, lineage, commentary, and practice.

This repository seeks to preserve that tradition by adhering to the following principles:

- Pramāṇa (authoritative evidence) takes precedence over opinion.
- Original scriptures take precedence over later interpretations.
- Traditional commentaries are preferred over speculative interpretations.
- Authenticity is valued above popularity.
- Multiple authentic traditions are respected.
- Every significant statement should be traceable to an identifiable source.

---

# Research Principles

## RP-001 — Pramāṇa First

Primary scriptures are always preferred over secondary or tertiary sources.

Whenever possible, research should begin from the original text rather than from summaries or modern interpretations.

---

## RP-002 — Scripture Before Interpretation

Whenever applicable, information should be presented in the following order:

1. Original Sanskrit
2. IAST Transliteration
3. Literal Translation
4. Contextual Translation
5. Traditional Commentary
6. Repository Summary

Interpretation must never be presented as scripture.

---

## RP-003 — Evidence Over Popularity

Popularity does not establish authenticity.

The following are **not considered evidence** by themselves:

- YouTube popularity
- Social media posts
- Viral articles
- Blog opinions
- Quote images
- Unverified internet claims

---

## RP-004 — Tradition-Aware Research

Sanātana Dharma contains multiple authentic sampradāyas.

Whenever legitimate doctrinal differences exist, the repository should:

- identify the sampradāya,
- identify the commentator,
- identify the source,
- avoid presenting one interpretation as universally accepted unless supported by broad traditional consensus.

---

## RP-005 — Transparency

Every note should clearly distinguish between:

- Scripture
- Commentary
- Interpretation
- Historical information
- Repository Summary

Readers should always know the origin of each idea.

# Translation Policy

Whenever practical, permanent notes should contain:

- Original Sanskrit
- IAST Transliteration
- Literal Translation
- Contextual Translation
- Traditional Explanation
- Repository Summary

If multiple translations differ significantly, the important differences should be documented.

---

# Commentary Policy

Traditional commentaries should always be attributed.

Example:

Bhagavad Gītā 2.47

- Śaṅkarācārya explains...
- Śrī Rāmānujācārya explains...
- Madhvācārya explains...

The Repository Summary should distinguish:

- points of agreement,
- points of divergence,
- and unresolved interpretations.

---

# Citation Standard

Whenever practical, every permanent note should record:

- Scripture
- Chapter
- Section
- Verse
- Publisher
- Edition
- Publication Year (if known)
- Translator (if applicable)
- Commentary (if applicable)

---

# Translation Preference

Whenever multiple editions exist, preference should generally follow:

1. Original Sanskrit
2. Traditional Sanskrit editions
3. Traditional translations
4. Critical editions
5. Modern scholarly translations

No translation should replace the original Sanskrit where textual precision is required.

---

# Handling Conflicting Interpretations

When multiple authentic interpretations exist:

- Present each fairly.
- Identify the associated tradition.
- Cite the supporting source.
- Avoid artificial harmonization.
- Distinguish established doctrine from later interpretation.

The purpose of the repository is to document authentic positions—not to declare winners.

---

# Editorial Research Standard

Every permanent note should answer the following questions:

1. What is the primary source?
2. Which edition is being used?
3. Which commentary is being referenced?
4. Which translation is being used?
5. Which statements are scriptural?
6. Which statements are interpretative?
7. Can the reader independently verify this information?

# Editorial Rule ER-001

Every permanent note in this repository should, wherever reasonably possible, be traceable to at least one **S1–S5** source.

Notes based solely on AI-generated content or unverified secondary sources shall not be considered complete until supported by appropriate references.
