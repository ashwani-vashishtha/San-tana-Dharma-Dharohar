# Purpose

This Constitution is the **Single Source of Truth (SSOT)** for the **Sanātana Dharma Dharohar** project.

It establishes the permanent governance framework of the repository and defines the principles under which all editorial work shall be conducted.

No governance document, editorial document, or repository content shall contradict this Constitution.

---

# Article 1 — Vision

To preserve, organize, and present the authentic knowledge traditions of **Sanātana Dharma** through a structured, evidence-based, non-sectarian repository that serves seekers, students, practitioners, and researchers for generations.

---

# Article 2 — Mission

The project shall:

- Build the Core Canon of 108 Essential Sādhana Resources.
- Preserve the breadth and diversity of Sanātana Dharma.
- Maintain editorial neutrality while respecting traditional perspectives.
- Encourage authentic study (Adhyayana) and practice (Sādhana).
- Create a sustainable and expandable knowledge repository.

---

# Article 3 — Scope

The repository includes, but is not limited to:

- Scriptures
- Philosophy
- Yoga
- Tantra & Āgama
- Bhakti Literature
- Stotras
- Sahasranāmas
- Sūktas
- Mantras
- Kavacas
- Ritual Traditions
- Saints & Spiritual Masters
- Sādhana Practices
- Knowledge Traditions
- Historical and Civilizational Literature

The repository does not seek to promote sectarian views, political ideology, or unsupported historical claims.

---

# Article 4 — Governance Hierarchy

The repository shall follow the following governance hierarchy.

1. Constitution
2. Decision Ledger
3. Core Canon Methodology
4. Core Canon Taxonomy
5. Core Canon Editorial Guide
6. Working Documents

In the event of a conflict, higher governance documents take precedence.

---

# Article 5 — Editorial Principles

The Core Canon shall:

- Represent the breadth of Sanātana Dharma.
- Remain non-sectarian.
- Use descriptive and academically neutral editorial language.
- Distinguish scriptural authority from traditional belief and later interpretation.
- Preserve historical authenticity.
- Remain stable once finalized.
- Represent major traditions proportionately without privileging any single Sampradāya.
- Represent philosophical schools through their principal textual works.
- Distinguish Primary Scriptures, Classical Commentaries, Practice Texts, and Modern Interpretations.
- Recognize both scriptural authority and enduring civilizational influence.
- Preserve literary traditions while avoiding unnecessary duplication.

---

# Article 6 — Evidence Hierarchy

Editorial decisions shall prioritize evidence in the following order:

1. Primary Scriptures
2. Traditional Commentaries
3. Classical Scholarly Works
4. Living Traditional Practice
5. Modern Academic Research

Whenever conflicts arise, higher levels of evidence shall normally take precedence.

---

# Article 7 — Canon Evaluation Criteria

A resource may qualify for inclusion through one or more of the following:

1. Scriptural Authority
2. Historical Significance
3. Philosophical Importance
4. Living Tradition
5. Civilizational Impact
6. Representative Value

No single criterion is necessarily sufficient in isolation.

---

# Article 8 — Canon Architecture

The Core Canon preserves **knowledge traditions**, not merely individual books.

The objective is to represent the complete intellectual structure of Sanātana Dharma through its foundational traditions.

Representation shall take precedence over enumeration.

---

# Article 9 — Repository Layers

The repository shall maintain three editorial layers.

## Layer I — Core Canon

The essential works required for understanding Sanātana Dharma.

## Layer II — Extended Canon

Works that substantially deepen understanding but are not indispensable to the Core Canon.

## Layer III — Research Archive

Historical, regional, manuscript, comparative, specialized, and research material preserved for completeness.

---

# Article 10 — Editorial Representation Model

Whenever applicable, each knowledge tradition should be represented through:

- Foundational Text
- Classical Commentary
- Advanced Literature

This representation model guides editorial evaluation but does not require every Core Canon entry to contain all three components.

---

# Article 11 — Editorial Principles for Inclusion

## Embedded Scripture Principle

Embedded scriptures shall normally be represented through their parent work.

Independent representation shall be reserved for works that have attained universally recognized independent authority.

Examples

**Independent Entry**

- Bhagavad Gītā

**Represented through Parent**

- Devī Māhātmya → Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa

---

## Non-Duplication Principle

Where the essential content of a work is already represented within another Core Canon entry, separate inclusion shall normally not be granted unless the work possesses independent scriptural, philosophical, historical, or devotional authority.

---

# Article 12 — Repository Standards

The repository shall:

- Use Markdown as the primary document format.
- Be maintained within Obsidian.
- Follow standardized metadata across governance documents.
- Use consistent Sanskrit transliteration where applicable.
- Maintain internal cross-references between related documents.
- Preserve long-term compatibility and readability.

---

# Article 13 — Document Governance

Every governance document shall contain:

- Version
- Status
- Created
- Last Updated
- Owner
- Related Documents
- Revision History

Changes to governance documents shall be synchronized across dependent documents where necessary.

---

# Article 14 — Amendment Process

Constitutional amendments shall normally follow this sequence:

1. Proposal
2. Review
3. Decision Ledger entry
4. Constitution update
5. Synchronization of dependent governance documents

Major constitutional changes should be minimized to preserve long-term stability.
