# Purpose

This methodology defines how foundational **Ādhāra Śilā (आधार शिला)** sources are discovered, evaluated, classified, justified, reviewed, approved, and maintained within the **Sanātana Dharma Dharohar** repository.

It governs editorial evaluation of foundational sources.

It does not replace the Constitution, Decision Ledger, Ādhāra Śilā Taxonomy, or Ādhāra Śilā Editorial Guide.

---

# Scope

This methodology applies only to **Ādhāra Śilā**, the repository's foundational knowledge layer.

It does not govern:

- Jīvanta Paramparā population;
- living-practice documentation;
- Dharma Sāgara expansion;
- Sādhaka Guidance System implementation.

The current repository architecture is authoritative. Historical Core Canon methodology material informs this reconstruction only where compatible with the current Constitution, Decision Ledger, taxonomy, and three-layer architecture.

---

# Role of Ādhāra Śilā

Ādhāra Śilā preserves the selected 108 foundational sources representing the scriptural, philosophical, devotional, liturgical, and contemplative architecture of Sanātana Dharma.

It is a curated foundational layer, not an exhaustive bibliography and not a curriculum.

Detailed architecture, section structure, and current progress are maintained in the Constitution, Decision Ledger, Ādhāra Śilā Taxonomy, Master Candidate List, and Ādhāra Śilā 108.

---

# Editorial Principles

Ādhāra Śilā evaluation is guided by the following principles.

## Pramāṇa First

Evaluation shall prioritize authoritative sources and the evidence hierarchy defined in the Constitution.

## Authenticity over Completeness

Ādhāra Śilā is intentionally selective. A source should not be included merely because it is popular, useful, or important in a general sense.

## Tradition-Aware and Non-Sectarian

Evaluation shall respect authentic traditional contexts while preserving non-sectarian editorial neutrality.

## Historical and Civilizational Significance

Sources that have shaped Sanātana Dharma across generations may receive stronger consideration when supported by evidence and aligned with the approved architecture.

## Representative Balance

Ādhāra Śilā should represent the breadth of Sanātana Dharma responsibly without privileging one Sampradāya or tradition beyond the evidence and architecture.

## Clear Editorial Justification

Every inclusion should be explainable through documented editorial reasoning.

# Evidence / Pramāṇa Orientation

Editorial evaluation is evidence-conscious and Pramāṇa-oriented.

The authoritative evidence hierarchy is defined in Constitution Article 6:

1. Primary Scriptures
2. Traditional Commentaries
3. Classical Scholarly Works
4. Living Traditional Practice
5. Modern Academic Research

This methodology does not create a competing evidence hierarchy, evidence class system, numerical scoring model, or automated evaluation process.

When evidence is incomplete or disputed, the uncertainty should be recorded rather than hidden.

---

# What Qualifies for Ādhāra Śilā

A distinct, identifiable textual work or textual corpus may qualify as an Ādhāra Śilā foundational source when it satisfies the approved architectural and editorial criteria.

The following do not automatically qualify as independent Ādhāra Śilā entries:

- ritual categories;
- thematic collections;
- generic practices;
- individual procedures;
- practice instructions.

Texts that contain practice instructions may be evaluated when their primary identity is as a foundational textual source. The existence of practical material inside a text does not turn Ādhāra Śilā into a practice-prescription layer.

Texts whose primary identity is philosophical, scriptural, devotional, liturgical, or contemplative should be evaluated according to their primary literary form, primary contribution, evidence, and approved taxonomy.

Texts or materials whose primary identity is living practice, ritual procedure, transmission context, or ongoing community expression should normally be considered under Jīvanta Paramparā or Dharma Sāgara according to the repository's three-layer architecture.

Embedded practice material should be documented descriptively where relevant, but it should not be extracted into unauthorized practice instructions.

---

# Evaluation Criteria

A source may qualify for inclusion through one or more of the Constitution Article 7 criteria:

1. Scriptural Authority
2. Historical Significance
3. Philosophical Importance
4. Living Tradition
5. Civilizational Impact
6. Representative Value

No single criterion is necessarily sufficient in isolation.

Evaluation should consider how the source contributes to the Ādhāra Śilā as a whole, including representation, balance, evidence, and fit within the approved taxonomy.

This methodology does not introduce additional mandatory scoring criteria and does not use numerical scores.

# Editorial Workflow

Ādhāra Śilā evaluation normally follows this workflow:

**Discovery -> Evidence Review -> Classification -> Evaluation -> Editorial Justification -> Review -> Owner Approval -> Inclusion / Synchronization -> Maintenance**

## Discovery

Potential sources are identified through scripture, traditional commentary, classical scholarship, living tradition, modern research, and repository editorial review.

Discovery records candidates for consideration. It does not imply inclusion.

## Evidence Review

Available evidence is reviewed according to the Constitution and relevant repository standards.

Evidence gaps, disputed claims, and uncertain attributions should be recorded for review.

## Classification

Candidate sources are classified using the current taxonomy and editorial classification framework.

Classification supports evaluation but does not by itself determine final inclusion.

## Evaluation

Candidates are evaluated against the approved criteria, current architecture, section scope, and repository-layer boundaries.

## Editorial Justification

Each proposed inclusion should explain why the source belongs, what it represents, and why it is foundational within the current architecture.

## Review

The candidate and its justification are reviewed for balance, representation, authenticity, overlap, omissions, evidence quality, and consistency with frozen decisions.

## Owner Approval

Final approval follows the repository's existing owner-review conventions.

Routine editorial work does not automatically require a Decision Ledger entry. Changes affecting governance, methodology, taxonomy, evidence standards, frozen architecture, or inclusion principles should follow the repository governance process.

## Inclusion / Synchronization

Approved changes are synchronized into the appropriate repository documents.

Repository population should distinguish candidate evaluation, final inclusion, and later freezing.

## Maintenance

Approved entries may later receive metadata corrections, source updates, status updates, and editorial corrections without silently reopening frozen architecture.

# Review and Approval

Ādhāra Śilā inclusion requires documented justification and review under repository governance.

Review should confirm:

- the source is a distinct identifiable textual work or textual corpus;
- evidence has been considered according to the Constitution;
- the proposed classification aligns with the taxonomy;
- the source contributes meaningfully to the foundational architecture;
- repository-layer boundaries are preserved;
- frozen sections and decisions are not reopened without authorization.

Approval of an individual inclusion is an editorial decision unless it changes governance, methodology, taxonomy, evidence standards, inclusion principles, or frozen architecture.

Methodology approval itself is a substantive governance matter and should be recorded according to existing repository conventions when explicitly instructed.

---

# Maintenance and Revision

Approved Ādhāra Śilā entries may require later maintenance, including:

- metadata correction;
- evidence or source updates;
- editorial status updates;
- citation improvements;
- spelling, transliteration, and formatting corrections;
- cross-reference corrections.

Maintenance must not silently reopen frozen architecture.

If a proposed change affects frozen architecture, inclusion principles, taxonomy, evidence standards, or this methodology, the existing governance process applies.

# Relationship to Jīvanta Paramparā and Dharma Sāgara

The repository maintains three editorial layers.

| Layer | Responsibility |
|-------|----------------|
| Ādhāra Śilā | Foundational knowledge and textual sources. |
| Jīvanta Paramparā | Living traditions, transmission context, practice-oriented material, and living-practice documentation. |
| Dharma Sāgara | Broader contextual, specialized, regional, historical, and research material. |

The same subject may appear across layers only when each layer has a distinct knowledge responsibility.

Ādhāra Śilā may include foundational texts that inform practice, but it does not prescribe practice.

Jīvanta Paramparā documents living practice and transmission contexts through its own approved framework.

Dharma Sāgara preserves materials that are important for broader study or research but not selected as part of the 108 foundational sources.

---

# Boundaries

Ādhāra Śilā methodology does not:

- prescribe Sādhana;
- provide initiation-dependent practice instructions;
- replace living teachers;
- certify spiritual attainment;
- define Guru or Diksha requirements;
- create new traditions;
- replace Jīvanta Paramparā;
- replace Dharma Sāgara;
- implement the Sādhaka Guidance System;
- create scoring systems;
- create automated approval logic.

Practice-oriented material belongs within the repository only through the appropriate approved layer and workflow.

---

# Canonical Knowledge and Curriculum

Ādhāra Śilā answers:

> What foundational sources belong in the repository's 108-source foundation?

A curriculum or learning pathway answers:

> In what order, context, or manner should a seeker study or approach those sources?

These are related but distinct questions.

Ādhāra Śilā determines foundational knowledge representation. It does not define a complete learning sequence, practice pathway, or Sādhaka Guidance System implementation.

---

# Repository Goal

Every proposed Ādhāra Śilā inclusion should be able to answer:

1. Why does this source belong?
2. What does this source represent?
3. Why is this source foundational within the current architecture?

If these questions cannot be answered with adequate evidence and editorial justification, the source should remain under review or be assigned to a more appropriate repository layer.
